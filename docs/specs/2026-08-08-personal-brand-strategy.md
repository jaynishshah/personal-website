# Personal brand strategy

**Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Status:** In progress — brand foundation, information architecture, content strategy, and visual foundation approved  
**Owner:** Jaynish Shah

This is the living strategy for Jaynish Shah's personal website. The brand foundation below is approved. Information architecture, content strategy, visual direction, and interaction principles will be added as they are developed and approved.

## Brand foundation

### Core idea

> Curious by nature, systemic in practice.

This describes the relationship between Jaynish's personal and professional identities:

- Curiosity determines what he notices and pursues.
- Systems thinking determines what he does with what he discovers.
- Progress is the outcome: finding connections, creating clarity, and moving meaningful initiatives forward.

### Positioning

Jaynish is a lead product designer specialising in design systems. He thrives in ambiguity, identifies opportunities with wider systemic impact, and helps teams turn fragmented problems into shared progress.

### Desired impression

The visitor should leave thinking:

> He clearly knows design systems, but he also seems observant, thoughtful, and interesting. I'd like to follow his work or work with him.

### Brand characteristics

- **Curious** — interested in how things work, connect, and came to be.
- **Discerning** — notices details and selects what is genuinely worth attention.
- **Pragmatic** — values progress and usefulness over theoretical perfection.
- **Generous** — shares useful thinking without positioning himself as the definitive authority.
- **Quietly playful** — creates moments of delight without turning the experience into a performance.

### Guiding principles

1. **Expertise leads; curiosity animates it.**  
   Design systems remain unmistakably central, but the site reveals the person behind the practice.

2. **Show ways of seeing, not only finished outputs.**  
   The distinctive value is Jaynish's judgment: what he noticed, why it mattered, and how he approached it.

3. **Express personality through taste, not updates.**  
   Photography, references, language, and selected details provide personal texture without creating a publishing obligation.

4. **Make playfulness discoverable.**  
   Small interactions, unexpected connections, and subtle visual rewards suit Jaynish better than loud animation or novelty navigation.

5. **Build something that ages well.**  
   The website should remain credible even when untouched for months. Nothing should depend on appearing constantly current.

### Strategic boundaries

The site should not become:

- A design-systems consultancy pitch.
- A continuously updated personal feed.
- A lifestyle scrapbook competing with Jaynish's professional work.
- A visually literal “design system” made entirely of grids, tokens, labels, and technical diagrams.
- An overly polished authority persona that removes uncertainty, humour, or humanity.

## Primary objective

The priority is recognition among design-systems practitioners. Future leadership, speaking, and writing opportunities should emerge from that reputation.

The website should establish three impressions, in this order:

1. Jaynish seems like an interesting person worth following or working with.
2. Jaynish is a serious design-systems leader.
3. Jaynish has a distinctive way of thinking about systems and design.

## Maintenance constraint

The website must not create another recurring content obligation. Case studies and essays remain the only deliberately published website content.

Personality should come from durable choices such as language, photography, visual references, and interaction details. Newsletter and Instagram may remain outbound destinations, but the website should not depend on embedded feeds or frequent updates.

## Information architecture

The information architecture remains intentionally small. The strategy changes what each page is responsible for rather than adding more destinations.

```text
Home
├── Work
│   └── Case study
├── Writing
│   └── Essay
└── About

External destinations
├── Newsletter
├── Instagram
├── LinkedIn
└── CV
```

### Primary navigation

The primary navigation is:

**Jaynish Shah · Work · Writing · About**

- The wordmark returns home.
- Work and Writing are the two forms of professional evidence.
- About carries Jaynish's story, perspective, and personal texture.
- Newsletter, social profiles, CV, and contact information live in the footer or relevant page contexts.
- The theme control may remain as utility navigation.

Newsletter should not compete with the three core destinations. It may receive a considered invitation on the homepage and in the footer.

### Home

The homepage is a compact front door rather than a portfolio feed or compressed CV. It should answer three questions quickly:

1. Who is Jaynish?
2. What is distinctive about his practice?
3. Where should the visitor go next?

It contains:

1. **Opening** — Jaynish's name, current practice, design-systems domain, and a human expression of his perspective. It introduces a person rather than advertising a service.
2. **Primary routes** — Work, Writing, and About remain visibly separate from the introductory prose while forming part of the same compact composition. Visitors should not need to notice links embedded in a paragraph to find the main destinations.
3. **Optional visual anchor** — a fixed photograph or photographic diagram may appear if it strengthens the composition. It is not a rotating feature or publishing slot.
4. **Elsewhere** — newsletter, Instagram, LinkedIn, and a direct contact route remain available without competing with the primary routes.

The homepage does not preview selected projects or recent essays. Professional evidence begins one deliberate step deeper in Work and Writing. The current résumé-style “Current / Previously” block moves to About.

### Work

**Purpose:** demonstrate how Jaynish operates within complex systems.

- A short positioning introduction.
- A deliberately selected case-study index.
- No filtering or taxonomy until the number of case studies makes it useful.
- Each case-study page stands alone for visitors arriving through a shared link.

The public label remains **Work**. The existing `/case-studies` route may remain unless there is a later reason to simplify URLs.

### Writing

**Purpose:** build recognition through useful ideas and perspective.

- One index containing all essays.
- Titles, summaries, dates, and lightweight topics.
- No separate Notes, Discoveries, or Newsletter archive.
- No filtering until the body of writing becomes large enough to need it.

The public label remains **Writing**, even if the existing `/blog` route stays.

### About

**Purpose:** make the professional identity feel personal, credible, and memorable.

It may contain:

- Jaynish's path from architecture to product and design systems.
- How curiosity and systems thinking connect in his practice.
- His approach to ambiguity, collaboration, and meaningful progress.
- A small, fixed selection of personal photography.
- A concise experience history.
- Glasgow and life outside work, without becoming a conventional hobbies list.
- CV, newsletter, social, and contact links.

Photography becomes part of the site's authorship and atmosphere, not a portfolio section that needs maintenance.

### Remove or absorb

- **Now:** remove it rather than maintaining a page whose value depends on freshness.
- **Discoveries:** keep discovery as a quality of the experience, not a content type.
- **Photography:** use it across Home and About rather than creating a gallery.
- **Practice or Principles page:** distribute the relevant thinking across Home, About, Work, and Writing.
- **Dedicated Newsletter page:** link directly to the existing newsletter.

### Content model

The website only needs two recurring content types:

- Case studies
- Essays

Everything personal remains evergreen page content. This keeps the architecture expressive without creating another publishing system.

## Content strategy

The content makes Jaynish's expertise useful and his personality perceptible. It reveals how he notices and thinks without requiring frequent personal updates.

| Surface | Primary job | Intended visitor response |
|---|---|---|
| Home | Establish recognition | “I understand what Jaynish is about.” |
| Work | Build trust | “He can navigate consequential systems work.” |
| Writing | Demonstrate generosity and perspective | “His thinking is worth following.” |
| About | Create affinity | “I understand the person behind the practice.” |

### Voice

The website speaks primarily in the **first person**. It is Jaynish's personal space rather than an institutional biography.

The voice is:

- **Direct:** clear language and concrete examples.
- **Observant:** describe what was noticed before explaining what was done.
- **Reflective:** acknowledge uncertainty, compromises, and changed thinking.
- **Generous:** help readers understand rather than attempting to impress them with expertise.
- **Lightly playful:** allow occasional unexpected phrases, captions, and asides.
- **Specific:** prefer precise detail over broad claims about leadership or impact.

Avoid consultancy language when it reads like a service proposition. Avoid presenting every piece of work as a smooth success story.

### Message hierarchy

Across the website, the ideas appear in this order:

1. Jaynish is a lead product designer focused on design systems.
2. Curiosity shapes what he notices and investigates.
3. Systems thinking helps him connect those discoveries and create progress.
4. His work demonstrates judgment within ambiguity.
5. His writing shares what he is learning and thinking.
6. Architecture, photography, objects, and interactions reveal the taste behind the practice.

“Curious by nature, systemic in practice” may appear explicitly once, but it should primarily be demonstrated rather than repeatedly used as a slogan.

### Home content

The opening introduces Jaynish, not only the value he provides to organisations. It includes:

- His name and design-systems focus.
- A concise expression of his way of working.
- Enough personal warmth to avoid reading like a consultancy homepage.
- A grounding detail such as Glasgow, architecture, or observation.

The homepage remains concise. It does not need project summaries, article summaries, a personal-lens section, or any “latest” content. Work, Writing, and About carry those responsibilities after the visitor chooses a route.

### Work content

Case studies reveal judgment through the following narrative rhythm:

1. What was happening?
2. What was unclear, fragmented, or constrained?
3. What did Jaynish notice?
4. What did he decide to prioritise?
5. What trade-offs did he accept?
6. How did the work move forward?
7. What changed?
8. What would he carry into future work?

Artifacts support the story rather than becoming the story. The tone remains collaborative and precise about Jaynish's contribution.

This is a content framework only. It does not determine which private or public projects become case studies.

### Writing content

- Publish when there is something worth developing, without implying a cadence.
- Allow technical essays, reflective practice pieces, and career stories to coexist.
- Use titles and summaries that surface the central tension or useful idea.
- Favour durable ideas over news-style commentary.
- Do not label the section “Latest” if freshness cannot be promised.
- Avoid empty promises such as “More coming soon.”

A small body of strong writing is more credible than a busy archive maintained out of obligation.

### About content

About uses curiosity as the thread connecting architecture, experimentation, product design, and design systems.

Personal details reveal how Jaynish sees rather than forming a hobbies inventory. Photography may be paired with brief captions about what caught his attention: repetition, material, light, scale, a threshold, an unexpected interaction, or an object worth keeping.

Experience belongs here in a concise form, with the CV available for visitors who want full detail.

### Photography and visual content

Jaynish's photography functions as **evidence of attention**.

- Use a fixed, edited selection rather than a live gallery.
- Prioritise architecture, texture, space, repetition, material, and unusual details.
- Let captions add meaning instead of merely naming the location.
- Mix professional artifacts with personal photography carefully so their roles remain clear.
- Avoid generic decorative stock imagery.

### Microcopy and personality

Playfulness may live in small places:

- Image captions
- Hover and focus responses
- Transitional links
- Empty and error states
- Footer language
- Occasional asides inside essays and case studies

Navigation and important actions remain plain and predictable. Personality rewards attention rather than obstructing it.

## Visual foundation

The visual design expresses Jaynish's taste. It does not need to illustrate or symbolise design systems.

The separation of responsibilities is deliberate:

- Professional positioning is established through language, selected work, case-study decisions, and information architecture.
- Visual design creates a distinctive, calm, and engaging setting for that content.
- Photography introduces personal texture and demonstrates Jaynish's attention.

### Palette and imagery

- Use a predominantly monochrome interface with one restrained highlight colour.
- Architectural photography remains in its natural colour. It is content rather than part of the interface palette.
- Do not apply a mandatory monochrome filter or force photographs to match a predetermined brand treatment.
- Choose the highlight colour after reviewing the photography so that it complements rather than competes with the images.
- Keep the photographic selection fixed and edited; its placement, scale, and cropping will be decided with the actual images.

### Circadian theme

Light and dark mode remain important, but they become part of a broader circadian experience rather than a binary utility setting. The website should recognise five atmospheric phases across the visitor's day:

1. Morning
2. High day
3. Golden hour
4. Twilight
5. Night

The phases respond to local daylight, including sunrise and sunset, when the visitor chooses to share device location. Solar timing should be calculated without retaining or transmitting precise coordinates. If location is unavailable or declined, use the device's local time and colour-scheme preference as a graceful fallback. A manual theme control must remain available and take precedence over automatic behaviour.

This is intended to be a memorable feature and an expression of curiosity, observation, and changing light. It must remain quiet in use: page structure, typography, and imagery do not move when the atmosphere changes; only the interface colour roles transition.

The circadian behaviour is designed to be discovered rather than announced. Do not explain it in the homepage copy or introduce an atmosphere badge, local-time display, onboarding prompt, or unsolicited location request. On a first visit, use the available device time and colour-scheme signals. The restrained theme control may reveal the option to follow local daylight more precisely; choosing that option is the moment to request location permission.

The exact five palettes, phase boundaries, transition behaviour, and names shown in the interface are deliberately deferred until they can be developed against Jaynish's selected photographs. The exploratory colours are not approved. Each eventual palette must preserve accessibility, keep photographs in natural colour, and feel like a related state of one identity rather than a separate theme.

### Article feature images

The preferred feature image for an essay is a photograph of a sketch made for that idea. The photograph should preserve the physical qualities of the process—paper, pencil or pen, natural light, shadows, edges, and surrounding surface—rather than recreating the sketch as a polished digital illustration.

When an article does not naturally suggest a sketch, use an architectural photograph or a photographed found object instead. The visual should have a real relationship to the article rather than acting as generic decoration.

- Use one feature image per article.
- Establish a recurring image proportion or presentation area while allowing each subject to remain distinct.
- Natural colour remains the default.
- A sketch may use the website's highlight colour when it suits the idea; this is optional rather than a template requirement.
- Feature images are created only when an essay is published and do not introduce a separate content cadence.
- Essential information contained in a photographed diagram must also be available in the article text and image description.

### Typography and composition

- Use ManvsOffice (the existing `MV Office` font files) for display typography, navigation, links, and interface text.
- Pair it with a restrained, contemporary reading serif for body copy, introductions, case-study narratives, and essays. Look for moderate contrast, crisp shapes, an even reading texture, and clarity at body sizes. Avoid nostalgic, overtly literary, ornamental, or high-fashion serif styles. The serif has a clear editorial role rather than appearing as occasional decorative emphasis.
- Select the final serif by testing it alongside ManvsOffice across the compact homepage introduction and longer article pages. Source Serif 4 is available in the codebase and serves as the practical baseline; another candidate should replace it only if it makes the pairing materially better.
- Create hierarchy through scale, weight, spacing, and placement.
- Use monochrome restraint, large direct typography, generous empty space, simple geometric composition, thin dividers, and prominent navigation as useful visual reference points.
- Avoid ornamental metadata, small labels, numbering, diagrams, or pseudo-technical devices whose main purpose is to make the website appear systematic.
- Do not make the website visually imitate a design system. Systemic practice should be proven by the content.

### Reference boundary

[Zeh Fernandes's website](https://zehfernandes.com/) is a reference for visual taste and selected layout qualities only: monochrome restraint, spatial confidence, typographic scale, simplicity, and navigation integrated into the page composition.

It is not being used as evidence that a particular visual language communicates systems thinking, and its layout should not be copied literally.

[PJ Onori's website](https://pjonori.design/) is a reference for the compactness of its introduction, the restraint of its writing index, and the simplicity of its article imagery. Jaynish's homepage should retain more explicit separation between Work, Writing, and About rather than adopting the reference's extreme minimalism.

[Noé Chagué's website](https://noechague-site.vercel.app/) is a reference for profile-like intimacy, conversational language, a quiet single-typeface hierarchy, and photographs that behave like personal or found objects. Its overlapping photo treatment is a useful interaction reference, not a prescribed composition; Jaynish's photographs remain in natural colour and their final placement will be decided with the images.

Do not borrow Noé's contributions graph, events feed, latest-status indicators, local-time display, or other continuously changing profile modules. They create a maintenance obligation and turn the homepage into a live dashboard, conflicting with the compact, evergreen front-door strategy.
